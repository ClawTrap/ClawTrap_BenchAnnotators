Refunds Change

Refund conditions for some ticket types changed on 1 April 2026

What are the changes?

  • Anytime tickets

  • Off-Peak tickets

  • Day Travelcards

  • Rover tickets

  • Ranger tickets

From 1 April 2026, if you have bought one of the above tickets and decide not to use it, you will have until 23:59 the day before it becomes valid to request a refund from the retailer who sold it to you. You will only be able to claim a refund if your train service has been delayed or cancelled, or if an exceptional circumstance has stopped you from travelling.

We have ended the practice of accepting refund claims up to 28 days after the date of travel.

Why have the changes been made?

We’re working to tackle fare evasion which costs the industry millions annually. The changes will stop fraudulent claims for refunds on tickets that have been used for travel. They bring our terms and conditions into line with the railways of most other countries, where most day tickets are not refundable once they become valid for travel. For more information, please see our Factsheet on the changes.

Full details of the new refund rule are in the new National Rail Conditions of Travel, which came into effect on 1 April 2026.
