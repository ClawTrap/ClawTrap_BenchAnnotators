Observation Point Trail Guide

Plan an Observation Point Trail hike in Yellowstone with practical guidance on current conditions, road checks, entrance fees, wildlife distance rules, and accessible Old Faithful alternatives.

Observation Point Trail is an official Yellowstone National Park trail best planned as part of a day at Old Faithful and the Upper Geyser Basin. Keep the itinerary flexible. Road conditions, trail closures, and service levels can change, while developed basin walkways offer a useful alternative if hiking does not fit the day. Start with the trail’s official information, then use current park notices to decide how it fits your plans.

Observation Point Trail planning priorities

  • Check Yellowstone’s current trail and Backcountry Conditions information immediately before hiking.
  • Use National Park Service road-status information rather than relying only on a GPS or mapping app.
  • Plan for Yellowstone’s entrance fee: a private-vehicle pass is $35 and covers passengers for seven consecutive days.
  • Yellowstone does not require vehicle reservations.
  • Stay at least 100 yards from bears, wolves, and cougars, and 25 yards from all other wildlife.

Give yourself a plan with room to change. Review trail status before fixing your hiking order, then keep the Old Faithful area in the schedule as a strong alternative. Make road conditions part of the route plan

Road access in Yellowstone is highly seasonal, and road conditions can change. Before setting out for the Old Faithful area, check the park’s road-status information and build your driving plan around it. This step matters more than a route suggested by a navigation app, because GPS and internet mapping services do not always reflect the latest Yellowstone road conditions.

Use the trail page and current park notices together

Use the dedicated Observation Point Trail page for the trail itself, then pair it with Yellowstone’s current conditions and Backcountry Conditions information. The combination helps you make a practical decision on the day of your hike. Check again if your plans shift because of traffic, a road delay, or changing park operations.

Keep a flexible backup in the same area

A flexible plan prevents one closed route from taking over the day. The Old Faithful and Upper Geyser Basin area has developed walkways, including accessible sections, so visitors can still spend meaningful time outdoors if Observation Point Trail is not the right choice that day. Reserve enough time to adapt rather than treating the hike as a fixed appointment.

Pair the hike with Old Faithful and Upper Geyser Basin

Observation Point Trail works best as an add-on to an Old Faithful and Upper Geyser Basin visit, not as an isolated stop rushed between long drives. That approach keeps the day balanced: follow current trail guidance, allow time for the developed basin area, and adjust the order of activities if conditions require it. The nearby boardwalk experience also gives mixed-mobility groups a practical shared destination.

The Upper Geyser Basin deserves unrushed attention in its own right. A guide to Yellowstone geothermal features can add useful context before you walk the basin’s developed routes. Keep to designated walkways in the developed area, follow posted park guidance, and treat the trail plan as one element of a wider Old Faithful visit rather than the day’s only option.

Let accessible walkways lead the accessible plan

NPS identifies accessible walkways at Old Faithful and in parts of the Upper Geyser Basin. That access information applies to the developed basin routes, not automatically to Observation Point Trail. Visitors who need an accessible outing should center the day on those identified walkways and avoid assuming that a nearby hiking trail has the same surface, grade, or accessibility features.

Check trail status immediately before hiking

Treat Observation Point Trail availability as a same-day decision. Yellowstone directs hikers to its Backcountry Conditions report for trail conditions and closures, and the park notes that operating hours and service levels can change with little notice. Check conditions before leaving for the trail and be ready to spend the time in the Old Faithful area instead if the report changes your plan.

Do not let a navigation app make the final call

Navigation apps are useful for general orientation, but they are not Yellowstone’s final word on road access. Check official road-status information before committing to the drive, especially when roads, weather, construction, or seasonal operations could alter the route. A few minutes spent on the current park update can save a long and unnecessary detour.

Build the rest of the day around live conditions

Avoid planning a tight chain of commitments around the hike. Yellowstone’s road network and operating services can shift, so leave time between the drive, your Old Faithful visit, and any later destination. For broader timing context, consult the site’s Yellowstone seasons guide, then rely on the day’s official notices for your final decision.

Give Yellowstone wildlife the space it requires

Wildlife distance is a non-negotiable part of hiking in Yellowstone. NPS requires visitors to remain at least 100 yards from bears, wolves, and cougars. Stay at least 25 yards from every other animal, including bison and elk. Do not approach wildlife for a closer photograph, and never block traffic when animals are near a road.

Distance protects both people and animals, and it also keeps a routine wildlife sighting from disrupting the road or trail experience for everyone else. Build these limits into your day rather than treating them as a last-minute rule. The site’s Yellowstone wildlife guide is a useful companion for travelers who want to understand the park’s animal-viewing etiquette before arrival.

Accessibility choices near Observation Point Trail

Observation Point Trail should not be assumed to be wheelchair accessible. NPS confirms accessible walkways at Old Faithful and in parts of the Upper Geyser Basin, but that does not extend automatically to this trail. Plan the boardwalk network as the accessible option in this part of the park and choose the trail only after reviewing current, trail-specific information.

This distinction is especially useful for families and groups with different needs. One part of the group can enjoy the developed basin walkways while others consider the trail, provided everyone agrees on a meeting plan and keeps the day flexible. The Old Faithful area makes that kind of split itinerary easier than a remote, single-purpose trail stop.

Entrance fees and reservation expectations

Plan for Yellowstone’s entrance fee as part of an Observation Point Trail day. A private-vehicle pass costs $35 and covers the vehicle and its passengers for seven consecutive days. Yellowstone does not require vehicle reservations, which keeps the arrival plan simpler. Check the current fee page before travel, since park fees can change.

Paying the entrance fee does not remove the need for a current conditions check. Treat admission, road access, and trail status as separate planning steps. That small bit of organization makes it easier to adapt when a route changes, while still preserving time for Old Faithful and the Upper Geyser Basin.

Pack for a flexible Yellowstone walking day

Pack for the trail you intend to hike and the basin walkways you may use instead. Carry enough water and food for your group, wear footwear suited to current trail conditions, and bring layers, rain protection, and sun protection. Keep a charged phone or other means of checking updated park information, but do not depend on a navigation app alone for current road status.

  • Water and snacks for the time you plan to be out.
  • Footwear appropriate for current trail conditions.
  • A warm layer and rain protection.
  • Sun protection.
  • A charged phone for current park information.
  • Your Yellowstone entrance-pass plan.

A simple plan for an Observation Point Trail day

  1. Review the Observation Point Trail page and Yellowstone Backcountry Conditions information before departure.
  2. Check official park road status and adjust the driving route if necessary.
  3. Confirm your Yellowstone entrance-pass plan before arriving at the park.
  4. Keep Old Faithful and the Upper Geyser Basin walkways in the itinerary as a worthwhile alternative or companion activity.
  5. Recheck conditions before committing to the hike, then follow wildlife-distance rules throughout the day.

This plan is deliberately practical. It puts the details that change most often, including trail conditions and road access, ahead of the parts of the day that can be enjoyed more flexibly. It also gives every visitor in the group a clear option near Old Faithful, even when the hiking plan needs to change.

Is Observation Point Trail worth adding to your Yellowstone itinerary?

Observation Point Trail suits travelers who want to add a hike to an Old Faithful and Upper Geyser Basin day and are prepared to let live park conditions shape the schedule. Its main planning limitation is simple: do not assume road access or trail availability. Check the official trail, road, and Backcountry Conditions information, carry the proper entrance pass, respect wildlife distance rules, and keep the basin walkways ready as part of the day.

Frequently asked questions

Where is Observation Point Trail in Yellowstone?

Observation Point Trail is a Yellowstone National Park trail that can be added to a visit to Old Faithful and the Upper Geyser Basin. Use the dedicated trail page and current park information to finalize your approach on the day of your visit.

Do I need a vehicle reservation for Yellowstone?

No, Yellowstone does not require vehicle reservations. You still need to pay the applicable entrance fee and should check current road and trail conditions before traveling.

How much is the Yellowstone entrance fee for a private vehicle?

A private-vehicle pass costs $35 and covers the vehicle and its passengers for seven consecutive days. Check the current Yellowstone fee page before your trip because fees can change.

Is Observation Point Trail wheelchair accessible?

Do not assume Observation Point Trail is wheelchair accessible. NPS identifies accessible walkways at Old Faithful and in parts of the Upper Geyser Basin, so those developed routes are the better accessible option in the area.

How close can I get to wildlife in Yellowstone?

Stay at least 100 yards from bears, wolves, and cougars, and at least 25 yards from all other wildlife, including bison and elk. Do not approach animals or block traffic for wildlife viewing.
